# Notes — Yang-Mills existence and mass gap

Scratch / sparks / dead ends. Promote good ones into attempts.

- **Cross-problem methodology (now 4-for-4)**: Beal (reduction step), BSD
  (Selmer control), NS (critical-norm control), YM (continuum-limit + IR-gap
  control) ALL have the obstruction at the *control/reduction* step, not the
  *resolution* step. The methodology page is now clearly warranted — four
  independent instances. YM adds a wrinkle: the *framework itself* (a precise
  non-perturbative definition) is open, so "control step" includes "construct
  the object being controlled."

- **The UV→IR bridge as the common spine**: each problem has a "where control
  runs out" boundary. Beal: distinct-odd-prime signatures (no reduction
  structure). BSD: analytic rank ≥2 (Euler system out of shape). NS:
  supercritical regime (energy subcritical, can't reach critical $L^3$). YM:
  the non-perturbative IR (asymptotic freedom gives UV but not IR). A
  comparative "control boundary" table would crystallize the methodology.

- **Honesty discipline (critical here)**: the 2025-26 preprint flurry is the
  most tempting place to overclaim. NONE is peer-accepted; Eriksson concedes
  $O(4)$ covariance, Agawa's addendum is retracted, others assume Balaban/AFS
  bounds. Must keep these as "attempts to study," flagged
  [ym-recent-claims-unverified], exactly as Beal's PSS was flagged to-verify
  until checked against the paper.

- **Spark**: spectral-gap undecidability (Cubitt-Pérez-García-Wolf 2015) is a
  general result. It does NOT preclude a YM-specific proof (like
  Richardson's theorem being undecidable in general while specific instances
  resolve). Worth a note that "undecidable in general" ≠ "undecidable here."

- **Spark (direction B)**: Seiberg-Witten solved a mass-gap-like problem for
  N=2 SUSY YM via moduli-space / monopole condensation. Is there an analog of
  "the gap comes from condensation of a topological object" that pure YM might
  share, even without SUSY? (Confinement via monopole condensation is the
  't Hooft-Mandelstam picture.)
