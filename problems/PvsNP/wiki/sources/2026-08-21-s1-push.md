## S1, stated precisely

**S1:** derandomize Ilango's randomized quasipolynomial-time Turing reduction from SAT to **`(AC⁰_d)-MCSP`** (depth-d formula MCSP) — i.e. convert the *randomized* NP-hardness (FOCS 2020, all constant d≥2) into a **deterministic** NP-hardness. Confirmed open: "Derandomizing the `(AC⁰_d)`-MCSP reduction: Open" (search summary of the FOCS 2020 paper).

S1 is the most tractable of the three Route-A sub-targets (S1–S3) named in the `routeA-push` source, because derandomization is a *well-developed toolkit* and — decisively — **a precedent for exactly this kind of derandomization already exists** (`[s1-hos18-precedent]`).

## The two randomness sources, precisely located

Ilango's FOCS 2020 reduction is an inductive "lifting" argument (depth-d → depth-(d+1)). The randomness enters in **two** distinct places, and derandomizability differs between them:

### Source 1 — the probabilistic Splitting Claim (Chebyshev / second-moment)
The "Splitting Claim" (in the lifting proof): using the **second-moment method (Chebyshev's inequality)**, a *random partition* of the subformulas into two groups yields, with positive probability, a partition where each group is a `.73`-one-sided non-deterministic approximation of `g` — yielding the contradiction.

- This is a **second-moment / variance** argument. Crucially: Chebyshev only needs **pairwise independence** (2nd moment = variance), and pairwise-independent distributions have seed `O(log n)`. So the *randomness complexity* of this step is low — only `O(log n)` random bits are structurally required. This is the *most derandomizable* form of probabilistic argument (unlike Valiant–Vazirani hashing, which is the famously hard-to-derandomize one — and this is *not* VV).

### Source 2 — sampling g from a Lupanov-based distribution (Lemma 26)
The inductive step samples the auxiliary function `g` from a distribution based on **Lupanov's near-optimal depth-3 construction** (Lemma 26). Here the randomness is over the *choice of `g` relative to the input `f`*, and the requirement is that `g` be "expensive" (large non-deterministic + one-sided-approx complexity) compared to `f`.

- This is an **existential-over-inputs** use of randomness: no single `g` works for all `f`, so one is sampled. Lupanov's theorem is *constructive* (gives explicit near-optimal circuits), which leaves a door open for a deterministic substitute, but the distribution-over-inputs aspect is the subtler part.

(The ITCS 2020 `MOCSP` reduction is a *separate* reduction — from 6-SetCover, randomness = uniform random truth table `T` + random partition `P`, one-sided error. It is *not* the FOCS 2020 `(AC⁰_d)-MCSP` reduction; S1 targets the latter. They share a "randomness encodes structure" flavor but have different sources.)

## The decisive precedent — HOS18 already did this `[s1-hos18-precedent]`

**Hirahara–Oliveira–Santhanam 2018 (`[hos-2018]`)** achieved **deterministic polynomial-time NP-hardness of `(DNF◦MOD_m)-MCSP`** by *derandomizing a two-stage randomized reduction*. The technique:
- The randomized stages were zero-error reductions whose conditions ("niceness", "scatteredness") are foolable by **ε-biased distributions** (Naor–Naor [NN93]), which fool `AND◦MOD_m` circuits with seed `O(log n + log(1/ε))`.
- Derandomization: **enumerate all PRG seeds** (only `2^{O(log n)} = poly(n)` of them) and check the conditions deterministically in polynomial time.

**This is the key honest fact for S1:** derandomizing a randomized MCSP-variant reduction to *deterministic polytime* is **not hypothetical — it has been done**, for the `(DNF◦MOD_m)` class. S1 is the *extension* of this achievement to the strictly harder `(AC⁰_d)-formula` class (formulas vs DNFs; depth-d vs depth-2; no MOD gates). `[s1-hos18-precedent]`

## The obstruction to the direct port `[s1-meta-vs-lowdegree]`

Why doesn't HOS18's Naor–Naor derandomization transfer immediately to Ilango's `(AC⁰_d)-MCSP` reduction?

- HOS18's randomness conditions ("niceness/scatteredness" of the set-cover lift) are **`AND◦MOD_m`-type / low-degree-polynomial / parity-type** tests — exactly what ε-biased distributions fool.
- Ilango's randomness conditions are **meta-computational / circuit-complexity-based**: the Splitting Claim's "each group is a `.73`-one-sided non-deterministic approximation of `g`" and the Lupanov-sampling's "`g` is expensive enough" are statements about **formula-complexity quantities**, not low-degree-polynomial tests.

`[s1-meta-vs-lowdegree]`: ε-biased (and small-bias / k-wise) distributions fool *symmetric / low-degree / parity* tests, **not** meta-computational circuit-complexity conditions. (This is the same flavor as the `[dilv-2024]` finding from the majmaj-push — small-bias fails threshold/symmetric-structure tests — but here the "test" is the circuit-complexity potential itself.) So the Naor–Naor trick does **not** directly fool Ilango's conditions. The direct port is blocked.

## The named derandomization mechanism — MOCE-with-oracle `[s1-chebyshev-moce-oracle]`

This is the genuinely forward step of the push. For **Source 1 (the Chebyshev Splitting Claim)**, there is a plausible derandomization mechanism that is *not obviously blocked*:

- **Second-moment ⇒ pairwise-independence suffices.** Chebyshev's inequality bounds the variance; a pairwise-independent distribution over the partition coins has seed `O(log n)` and inherits the same concentration. So only `O(log n)` random bits are needed — enumerable.
- **The blocker for the method of conditional expectations (MOCE) is *evaluability of the potential*.** MOCE derandomizes by fixing coins one at a time to keep the conditional probability of "good partition" ≥ its expectation. This requires *computing* the conditional probability — which is the partition-quality, a circuit-complexity (formula-approximation) quantity.
- **The crack: in a Turing reduction, the oracle evaluates the potential.** Ilango's reduction is a *Turing reduction with an oracle* for `(AC⁰_d)-MCSP`. The partition-quality potential ("is group `i` a `.73`-one-sided approx of `g`?") is itself a formula-complexity question — expressible as a `(AC⁰_{d-1})`-MCSP-type query on `g`'s truth table. **By the inductive d-1↔d structure of Ilango's own proof**, the `d`-oracle can plausibly simulate the `(d-1)`-queries the potential needs. If so, MOCE runs *using the oracle* as the potential-evaluator, enumerating the `2^{O(log n)}` pairwise-independent seeds — yielding a **deterministic Turing reduction**.

`[s1-chebyshev-moce-oracle]`: the plausible derandomization of Source 1 is **method-of-conditional-expectations with the reduction's own oracle as the potential evaluator**, exploiting (i) pairwise-independence suffices for Chebyshev (short seed, enumerable) and (ii) the Turing-reduction oracle + the inductive d-1↔d structure make the partition-quality potential *evaluable via oracle*. This is a real, named, non-blocked mechanism — **not a completed derandomization**, because I have NOT verified against the full FOCS 2020 proof that the potential is actually expressible as an oracle query of the handled class (flagged below).

For **Source 2 (Lupanov-sampling of g)**: Lupanov's theorem is *constructive* (explicit near-optimal depth-3 circuits), so a deterministic `g` *might* substitute the sampled one — but the existential-over-inputs aspect (no single `g` works for all `f`) is the subtle part and is the harder, less-mechanized source. Flagged as the second, weaker-understood half.

## The honest gap-structure — S1 is necessary but insufficient for Route A `[s1-necessary-insufficient]`

Even a *fully successful* S1 — deterministic NP-hardness of `(AC⁰_d)-MCSP` — is still:

| Gap axis | Ilango (after S1) | Route A target |
|---|---|---|
| randomized → deterministic | **closed by S1** | deterministic |
| quasipoly-time → AC⁰-time | open | AC⁰-time (logtime-uniform) |
| Turing → many-one | open | many-one |
| restricted-class (depth-d formula) → original MCSP | open | original MCSP |

`[s1-necessary-insufficient]`: S1 closes **only the randomized→deterministic axis**. Route A (`[route-a-route-b]`, Murray–Williams Thm 1.8 `[murray-williams-2017]`) additionally requires closing the quasipoly→AC⁰, Turing→many-one, and restricted→original gaps. So a full S1 is **necessary-but-insufficient** for P≠NP: it would be a real advance (deterministic NP-hardness of a constant-depth-formula MCSP variant — strengthening `[ilango-2020]` and extending `[hos-2018]`), but it does *not* reach the uniform-AC⁰-many-one-on-original-MCSP rung that yields NP ⊄ P/poly. The honest framing: S1 is a *genuine publishable-class result if completed*, and the most tractable Route-A-adjacent step, but it is a step *toward* a step toward P≠NP, not a step to P≠NP itself.

1. **S1 precisely located** `[ilango-2020]`: derandomize Ilango's FOCS 2020 randomized quasipoly-time Turing reduction for `(AC⁰_d)-MCSP`; confirmed open.
2. **Two distinct randomness sources** pinned: the Chebyshev/second-moment Splitting Claim (low-randomness, pairwise-independence-suffices — the derandomizable kind, *not* Valiant–Vazirani) and the Lupanov-distribution sampling of `g` (existential-over-inputs — the harder kind).
3. **A real precedent** `[s1-hos18-precedent]`: HOS18 *already* derandomized a randomized MCSP-variant reduction to deterministic polytime (`(DNF◦MOD_m)-MCSP` via Naor–Naor ε-biased distributions). Derandomizing restricted-class MCSP reductions is achievable in principle; S1 = extending it to the harder `(AC⁰_d)-formula` class.
4. **The exact obstruction to the direct port** `[s1-meta-vs-lowdegree]`: ε-biased distributions fool low-degree/parity tests, not the meta-computational circuit-complexity conditions Ilango's reduction uses. (Same flavor as `[dilv-2024]`.)
5. **A named, plausible, non-blocked derandomization mechanism** `[s1-chebyshev-moce-oracle]`: for the Chebyshev split, **method-of-conditional-expectations using the reduction's own oracle as the potential evaluator**, exploiting pairwise-independence-suffices (short enumerable seed) + the Turing-oracle + the inductive d-1↔d structure. **Not completed** — the potential's expressibility as a handled-class oracle query is unverified against the full proof (flagged). The Lupanov-sampling source is the weaker-understood second half (constructive Lupanov is a door, the existential-over-inputs is the obstacle).
6. **Honest bound** `[s1-necessary-insufficient]`: even a full S1 closes only the randomized→deterministic axis; Route A still needs quasipoly→AC⁰, Turing→many-one, restricted→original. S1 is a publishable-class result and the most tractable Route-A-adjacent step, but necessary-but-insufficient for P≠NP.
