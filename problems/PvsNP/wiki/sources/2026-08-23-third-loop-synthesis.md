## The net structural product: a 2-D wall compressed to a 1-D barrier

The loop's net product is that the S1.a wall, which entered as a **two-dimensional** "cross two independent gates" barrier, exits as a **one-dimensional** "hit one balanced point" barrier:

- **The two gates are not independent.** Gate 1 (recognizability) is automatic for any successful Lupanov-style reduction (Cycle 7) and is (likely) re-tightened only via a recognizable variation that would itself reduce to (A) (Cycle 8); Gate 2 (the tight window) and the Gate-1 residual are coupled through ONE shared feature — the tight-window upper bound, the linchpin (Cycle 8). The two-gate/linchpin structure is then confirmed from inside the lifting theorem as the two intrinsic requirements on g (Cycle 9). So the wall is not "solve two things"; it is "solve one thing (A) that has two coupled sides."
- **(A) is a single balanced point, not a conjunction of two separately-achievable properties.** `[two-dim-to-one-dim-wall]` The naive decomposition "find an explicit hard function, then make it small-gap" (or the reverse) is DOOMED by the tension (Cycle 9): adjusting either axis moves the other the wrong way (verbatim: expense up ⟹ gap up). The construction must be **simultaneous / holistic** — hardness and small-gap must emerge from the *same* structural feature, as they do in Lupanov (the near-optimal CNF structure simultaneously yields small gap AND random-like hardness). `[balanced-point-non-compositional]`: a balanced-point barrier cannot be approached by solving the two sides separately and combining; the tension forbids composition. This is genuinely **optimistic in dimension** (one object, not two) and genuinely **pessimistic in method** (no compositional route; the construction must be holistic).

## What the compression reveals that was not visible at the start

Two things the second loop's "two independent gates" framing obscured, now visible:

1. **The wall's binding barrier is a single task (A) with one linchpin feature.** At the start of the loop, a breakthrough looked like "cross Gate 1 AND Gate 2" — two independent crossings, suggesting two independent ideas were needed. It is now: **construct an explicit function at the balanced point of {expensive (antiprox lower bound) ∧ small AND_{d−1}/OR_d gap (tight-window upper bound)}** — one object, one construction. The linchpin (the tight-window upper bound = the small-gap condition) is the single feature coupling the two former gates.

2. **Gate 1 is not a binding gate at all; it is a soft filter, discharged from three independent directions.** `[gate-1-soft-three-directions]` (a) Williams' (⇒) non-targetability — the reduction's *specific* g is not the function Williams' guaranteed recognizable property accepts (Cycle 6); (b) Ilango's automaticity — any *successful* deterministic many-one reduction automatically uses a non-recognizable LB collection, so Gate 1 is a consequence of success, not an independent obstacle (Cycle 7); (c) recognizable-variation one-sidedness — the recognizable natural properties that exist (CWY23) fail the tight-window upper bound and so cannot substitute in the reduction (Cycle 8). Gate 1 carries a residual — the "(likely)" hedges (Ilango's "(likely) loses the ability"; CWY23 web-confirmed not PDF-line-verified) — but is no longer a binding gate.

## The one new falsifiable target the compression surfaces — and why it is (still) circular to (A)

The balanced-point characterization sharpens (A) into a precise falsifiable target: `[decouple-expense-from-gap]` — **a construction in which the expense (lower-bound hardness) and the small-gap (tight-window upper bound) are not coupled** (a function family where increasing hardness does NOT widen the AND_{d−1}/OR_d gap). Lupanov couples them (the tension, observed verbatim); a construction that decoupled them would allow one to dial up hardness without violating the tight window, solving (A).

But this target is **(still) circular to (A)**: a decoupled explicit family with a tight DNF complexity in the window IS an explicit tight-window DNF LB = (A). So the compression surfaces a *sharper description* of the one lever, not a *new* lever. The honest content is the sharpening: (A) is not merely "an explicit hard function" (parity) — which would be a recognizable, natural, large-gap function and is ruled out — nor "an explicit small-gap function" (easy) — which is not expensive — but a function at the specific balanced point, and the *decoupling* of expense from gap is the precise constructive challenge. `[a-is-a-balanced-point]` is the load-bearing re-description.

## The residual hedge on the compression's own obstruction `[tension-is-lupanov-observed-not-proven-inherent]`

The loop's every soft-discharge carried a "(likely)" hedge. The final compression's own obstruction — the tension between expense and small-gap — carries one too, and it must be stated honestly. The tension is observed **verbatim for Lupanov** (Ilango §1.3, lines 385–387: "as g gets more expensive [the gap] also gets larger"). It is **NOT proven inherent to ALL explicit balanced-point constructions** — it is a property of the known (Lupanov) method. Whether a cleverly designed explicit family could decouple expense from gap (the `[decouple-expense-from-gap]` target) is exactly the open question; the tension suggests this is hard, but does **not** prove it impossible. So, parallel to how (B) was (likely)-non-independent (Cycle 8), the tension is a (likely)-inherent-but-not-proven obstruction. The frontier statement is: (A) = construct an explicit function at the balanced point; the tension (Lupanov-observed) suggests this is hard; a decoupling construction (if one exists) would solve (A). No impossibility is claimed — `[honest-ceiling]`.
