## Why the mining program is the honest next target (reframed by the S2 unifying diagnosis)

The S2 unifying diagnosis `[witness-needs-explicit-lb]` found that **both** Route-A-adjacent sub-attacks (S1.a, S2) require *explicitly constructing a NEW lower-bound-carrying witness* — which is the circuit-lower-bound problem itself (the natural-proofs frontier). That diagnosis redirects honest near-term leverage *away* from uniformizing existing meta-complexity variants (frontier-hard) toward the **constructive Williams side**: the mining program, where an **EXISTING** lower bound (HMPT) is to be *converted* into a derandomization/CAPP, rather than a new LB being proved. This source takes the mining program to the primary literature: locates its framework, raises a naive seam, and **VERIFIES the obstruction** to the direct route.

## The framework, precisely (from the primary literature)

- **The mining framing (Chen-Williams CCC 2019).** Non-trivial CAPP for poly(n)-size **THR◦THR** is equivalent (Williams-method sense) to such algorithms for **THR◦MAJ** (constant error) and **MAJ◦MAJ** (1/poly error). They state explicitly: since **exponential lower bounds for MAJ◦MAJ and THR◦MAJ are already known** (HMPT — Hajnal-Maass-Pudlák-Szegedy-Turán JCSS 1993/FOCS 1987; Goldmann-Håstad-Razborov 1992), "mining those known lower bounds for **algorithmic techniques**" could yield the missing CAPP and hence a THR◦THR lower bound. The LB is in hand; the *algorithm* (CAPP) is the missing half. Structure Lemma II: every s-size THR◦THR ≡ DOR◦MAJ◦MAJ with 2^{O(εn)} fan-in and s^{O(1/ε)}-size subcircuits — the reduction to MAJ◦MAJ needs a large-fan-in **DOR (gap-OR)**, which is why the **PCPP-based strengthening** (CAPP for OR₂◦C / AND₂◦C / ⊕₂◦C suffices) is the actual lever, not a direct MAJ◦MAJ CAPP.
- **The exact LB target (Chen-Ren STOC 2020, Thm 1.13 / ECCC TR20-010).** A non-trivial **CAPP for poly(n)-size MAJ◦MAJ** (2^n/n^{ω(1)} time, 1/poly(n) additive error) ⟹ **NEXP ⊄ MAJ◦MAJ◦MAJ = TC⁰₃** (worst-case, via the Discriminator Lemma). Separately, a non-trivial CAPP for THR◦THR (constant error) ⟹ NEXP ⊄ THR◦THR.
- **The within-reach lever (Tell STOC 2018, arXiv:1709.07635).** First *quantified* derandomization of TC⁰ (depth d>2): a PRG with seed Õ(log n) for linear-threshold circuits of **n^{1+exp(−d)} wires**; a modest improvement to **n^{1+O(1/d)} wires** ⟹ non-trivial *standard* derandomization of all TC⁰ ⟹ NEXP ⊄ TC⁰. For depth-2, a PRG with seed Õ(log n) for n^{3/2−Ω(1)}-wire circuits (Kane-Williams). The lever is the **wire-count** exponent (c>1 in n^{1+c^{−d}}) — `[tell-2018-quantified]`; a **promise** derandomization (only ≤n^{1+exp(−d)}-wire circuits), **not** the full poly-size CAPP Chen-Ren needs. This is the `[chen-tell-2019]` bootstrap engine.
- **Latest frontier (Chen-Tal-Wang STOC 2025).** Superquadratic (n^{2.5−ε}) THR◦THR worst-case LB via a 2^n − n^{Ω(ε)}-time CAPP for **XOR₂ of two n^{2.5−ε}-size THR◦THR** circuits (improving Tamaki 2016 / Alman-Chan-Williams 2016's n^{2−o(1)}). Confirms the Williams method is *actively* progressing on this exact class, but a **NEXP ⊄ THR◦THR** (and TC⁰₃) separation remains open.

## The seam — raised, then VERIFIED `[mining-nw-seam]` `[mining-worst-vs-average]`

**The naive question (raised):** why does the standard **Nisan–Wigderson / Impagliazzo-Wigderson hardness-to-randomness** reconstruction (LB for class C ⟹ PRG fooling C ⟹ CAPP for C) not *directly* convert HMPT's exponential MAJ◦MAJ LB into the missing MAJ◦MAJ CAPP? A naive parameter calculation (mine, initially unverified) suggested: HMPT gives f hard for MAJ◦MAJ of size 2^{(1/2−ε)ℓ}; NW from hardness 2^{δℓ} fools size-2^{(δ/c)ℓ} circuits, output 2^{(δ/c)ℓ} bits, seed d=O(ℓ); to fool poly(n)-size MAJ◦MAJ on n bits to 1/poly error needs ℓ=Θ(log n), seed d=O(log n), hence a **poly-time CAPP** — which (via Chen-Ren) would already give **NEXP ⊄ THR◦THR and TC⁰₃**, a known-open target. Since this is not a known result, the naive calc had to be wrong; the contradiction was the finding.

**The verified answer `[mining-worst-vs-average]` (located against the primary literature):** the error was conflating **worst-case** with **average-case** hardness.
1. **HMPT is a WORST-CASE lower bound** — no small MAJ◦MAJ circuit computes f on *all* inputs. It says nothing about computing f on, say, a 1/poly-biased majority of inputs.
2. **NW/IW requires AVERAGE-CASE hardness** — a function no small circuit computes on more than a (1/2+ε) fraction of inputs. The NW generator's fooling is proved via a hybrid/list-decoding argument that needs *average-case* hardness of f, not worst-case. (My naive calc plugged worst-case hardness into a theorem that needs average-case — the error.)
3. **Converting worst-case→average-case costs (and drifts class).** Hardness amplification (the Impagliazzo-Wigderson XOR lemma / direct-product) turns a worst-case-hard f into an average-case-hard f' — but: (a) **overhead** — amplification blows up the input length (XOR of k copies → kℓ bits) and costs exponent; (b) **class drift** — the predictor circuit for the amplified function (the NW hybrid predictor applied to f'=XOR-of-f) is an **XOR of MAJ◦MAJ predictors ≈ XOR◦MAJ◦MAJ, a DEEPER class**, not plain MAJ◦MAJ, which is **not closed under the top XOR**; so the amplified hardness is against a *larger* class, defeating the class-relativity that would fool MAJ◦MAJ itself; (c) **structure** — the efficient/uniform amplification route (Chen-Rothblum-Tell-Yogev JACM 2023) needs a **well-structured** hard function (downward self-reducible AND sample-aided worst-case-to-average-case reducible, via arithmetization of a PSPACE-complete problem); a generic LB like HMPT provides **no such structured hard function**. The NW generator also has an "inherent efficiency limitation" for sub-exponential hardness thresholds (Impagliazzo-Shaltiel-Wigderson FOCS 1999), partially addressed only by recursive constructions.
4. **The direction is one-way (CAPP→LB, not LB→CAPP).** The known Williams/Chen-Williams/Chen-Ren connections all go **CAPP algorithm ⟹ lower bound** (and strong average-case LB), NOT the reverse. An **average-case** LB for MAJ◦MAJ is, via Chen-Ren Thm 1.1 (CAPP for AND₄◦C ⟹ NE cannot be (1/2+1/poly)-approximated by C), essentially *equivalent to* (at least as hard as) the CAPP itself — so there is no shortcut from a worst-case LB to an average-case LB to a CAPP; the average-case LB *is* the open problem.

**Net verified obstruction:** the direct NW route from HMPT is blocked at the worst-case→average-case step: amplification (overhead + class drift to a deeper class + need for a structured hard function) does not yield an average-case-hard function for plain MAJ◦MAJ at the needed size, and an average-case MAJ◦MAJ LB is itself the open problem. This is precisely why the field reports the target as "within reach" (via the *promise*/wire-count route `[tell-2018-quantified]`, which relaxes the requirement — it derandomizes only a *promise* of few-wire circuits and so needs strictly less than full average-case hardness) rather than done. `[mining-nw-seam]` = the worst-case/average-case + class-drift obstruction; `[mining-worst-vs-average]` = the named core.

## The deeper unification (this is the genuinely novel diagnostic)

The mining-program obstruction is **the same obstruction** as the meta-complexity S1.a/S2 obstruction `[witness-needs-explicit-lb]`, seen from the constructive (Williams) side:
- **Meta-complexity side (S1.a, S2):** derandomizing/uniformizing needs an explicitly-constructed **lower-bound-carrying witness** (irritable T / Lupanov g) for a *restricted class*; non-uniformity/randomness supplies it *probabilistically* (existence by counting/second-moment); explicit construction = the circuit-LB problem (natural-proofs frontier).
- **Williams side (mining program):** derandomizing MAJ◦MAJ needs an explicitly-constructed **average-case-hard function for the restricted class MAJ◦MAJ**; HMPT supplies only a *worst-case* LB; converting worst-case→average-case (amplification) drifts to a deeper class / needs structure = again the circuit-LB problem.

**Unified statement `[witness-needs-explicit-lb]` (now spanning both routes):** *both* the meta-complexity reduction-uniformization route and the Williams mining route require, at their core, an **explicit, average-case (or witness-carrying) lower bound for a restricted circuit class**. In both, a probabilistic/existential argument supplies the object cheaply (non-uniform advice / random T / worst-case LB) but the **explicit construction is the circuit-lower-bound problem itself** (natural-proofs frontier). The two routes fail at the SAME wall, approached from opposite sides: meta-complexity needs the LB to uniformize a reduction; Williams needs the average-case LB to build a derandomization. The non-cryptographic, structural nature of Route A's resistance `[route-a-dual-locality]` and the worst-case-vs-average-case wall of the mining program are two faces of one fact: **we do not know how to explicitly construct high-complexity (lower-bound-carrying / average-case-hard) objects for restricted classes, and the only known ways to obtain them (non-uniformity, randomness, worst-case LBs) are precisely the cheap-but-insufficient inputs that the derandomization must replace.**

This unification is itself the honest deliverable: it says the *entire* current frontier — meta-complexity Route A *and* the Williams mining program — is governed by ONE obstruction (explicit restricted-class average-case/LB witnesses), approached from two directions, and that a single idea that produces such a witness for *any* one restricted class (in a derandomization-usable form) would advance both. It also sharpens *where not to look*: no amount of cleverness in the reduction (Route A) or the PRG construction (mining) helps if the witness isn't there; the lever is the witness-construction (or, for the within-reach near-term, Tell's promise relaxation that lowers the witness requirement).

## Refinement (verified, 2026-08-21, second pass) — the obstruction is QUANTITATIVE+REGIME, and the discrepancy→size-not-correlation point is the precise reason the naive calc fails

A second pass tested the just-recorded obstruction by asking the most dangerous question: **do any known depth-2 threshold lower bounds give *average-case (correlation)* hardness directly** (which would bypass the lossy worst-case→average-case amplification and open the seam)? The answer, verified against primary sources, REFINES (does not refute) the obstruction — and is more precise than "worst-case vs average-case":

1. **Explicit average-case (correlation) LBs for depth-2 threshold DO exist — but only at the few-wire / superquadratic-wire regime, with polynomial correlation.**
   - **CSS16** (Chen-Santhanam-Srinivasan, ToC 2018, "Average-Case Lower Bounds and Satisfiability Algorithms for Small Threshold Circuits"): for each depth d, Parity has correlation ≤ n^{−ε_d} with depth-d threshold circuits of ≤ n^{1+ε_d} **wires**; the Generalized Andreev function has correlation ≤ exp(−n^{ε_d}). Depth-2 tight (Thm 4.1): any depth-2 threshold circuit with ≤ n^{1+ε} wires (ε<1/2) has correlation ≤ n^{−Ω(γ)} with Parity, γ=1/2−ε — and this is **tight** (Parity is well-approximated by depth-2 with O(√n) gates / O(n^{1.5}) wires). `[css-2018]`
   - **Kane-Williams** (STOC 2016, "Super-linear gate and super-quadratic wire lower bounds"): Andreev's function cannot be computed on a (1/2+ε)-fraction by depth-2 THR◦THR of o(n^{1.5}/log³ n) **gates** / o(n^{2.5}/log^{3.5} n) **wires** — average-case, but superlinear-gate/superquadratic-wire, **not** exponential size. `[kane-williams-2016]`
   So "no average-case LB exists for depth-2 threshold" is **too blunt** — they exist, but are (i) **few-wire/superquadratic** (not full poly-size) and (ii) **polynomial correlation** (n^{−ε}, not 2^{−Ω(ℓ)}).

2. **The exponential HMPT/Forster bounds are SIZE bounds, not average-case-CORRELATION — the precise reason the naive NW-from-HMPT-average-case calc was wrong.** Per O'Donnell's depth-2-TC⁰ note: HMPT (IP2 ∉ MAJ◦THR) uses **discrepancy / randomized communication complexity**; Forster (IP2 ∉ THR◦MAJ) uses **sign-rank / average margin** (unbounded-error, barely above 1/2). Discrepancy bounds the **correlation with rectangles**; a size-s MAJ◦THR circuit is **richer than s rectangles** (it is a threshold of s thresholds), so discrepancy does **not** bound the average-case **correlation** of a size-2^{Ω(n)} MAJ◦THR circuit with IP2 — the correlation can be as large as ~s·2^{−n/2}, which at s=2^{Ω(n)} is **not small**. Thus HMPT yields a **worst-case SIZE** lower bound (any sub-2^{Ω(n)} MAJ◦THR fails to compute IP2 on *all* inputs), **not** an average-case **correlation** bound at that size. The earlier second-pass thought ("discrepancy ⟹ average-case, so NW directly works") was the over-optimistic misreading — discrepancy ⟹ average-case **against rectangles**, and composed threshold circuits exceed rectangles. (Forster's sign-rank similarly bounds approximability by a **single** threshold / unbounded-error protocol, depth-1, and does not lift to depth-2 average-case correlation.) This is the exact, primary-source-grounded reason the naive NW calc fails, sharper than the first pass's "amplification overhead + class drift" (which remains a valid but secondary description of the worst-case→average-case *conversion* route).

3. **Refined seam statement `[mining-worst-vs-average]` (now quantitative + regime-precise).** The mining obstruction is **not** "no average-case LB" but the **quantitative+regime gap**: known average-case (correlation) LBs for depth-2 threshold are (i) **few-wire (n^{1+ε}) / superquadratic** and (ii) **polynomial correlation (n^{−ε})**; NW for a **full poly-size** MAJ◦MAJ CAPP (Chen-Ren) needs average-case hardness at the **full poly-size regime with ≤1/poly correlation**, which after the NW predictor loss requires **exponential-size hardness with strong (2^{−Ω(ℓ)}) correlation** — i.e., an **exponential average-case-correlation** LB for full poly-size MAJ◦MAJ, which is **open** (the known exponential bounds are worst-case-size, not average-case-correlation; the known average-case bounds are few-wire/polynomial). The two missing ingredients coincide: (a) an explicit function with exponential average-case correlation against full poly-size MAJ◦MAJ, or (b) extending CSS16's few-wire average-case LB past the n^{1+ε}-wire barrier.

4. **This CONFIRMS Tell's lever is exactly the right seam — and identifies it with CSS16's wire frontier.** CSS16's average-case LB lives precisely at the **few-wire (n^{1+ε})** regime = Tell's **promise** derandomization regime `[tell-2018-quantified]`. Tell's open improvement (n^{1+exp(−d)} → n^{1+O(1/d)} wires) is **the same question** as extending CSS16's average-case (correlation) LB to more wires. So the near-term lever is not a separate idea — it IS the CSS16 wire-count frontier, and Tell's quantified derandomization + CSS16's correlation LB are two faces of the same few-wire average-case theory. The genuine-open shape for a breakthrough: an **exponential average-case-correlation LB for full poly-size MAJ◦MAJ** (open; would solve the mining program ⟹ NEXP ⊄ THR◦THR + TC⁰₃), or pushing the CSS16/Tell wire frontier past its current barrier.

**Honest status of the test:** I raised the most dangerous counter-question to my own obstruction (does a known average-case LB for depth-2 threshold open the seam?) and verified it does **not** — the known average-case LBs are few-wire/polynomial, the exponential LBs are worst-case-size (discrepancy gives size, not correlation, for composed circuits). The seam **holds**, now characterized with primary-source precision rather than the first pass's amplification framing. No opening found; no breakthrough; the obstruction is refined and the lever (CSS16/Tell wire frontier) is confirmed as the exact, correctly-located near-term seam. `[honest-ceiling]` upheld — the product of this pass is a **verified refinement** (too-blunt "worst-case vs average-case" → precise "few-wire/polynomial-correlation vs exponential-size/strong-correlation; discrepancy→size-not-correlation for composed circuits"), not a result.
