# Notes — Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness

Scratch / sparks / dead ends. Promote good ones into attempts.

- **Cross-problem methodology (now 3-for-3)**: Beal (reduction step), BSD
  (Selmer control step), NS (critical-norm control step) ALL have the
  obstruction at the *control/reduction* step, not the *resolution* step. This
  is a genuine reusable research strategy — worth promoting to a dedicated
  methodology page that names the pattern and the three instances.

- **The "2D vs 3D = Serrin numbers equal vs not" fact** is the cleanest
  single-sentence explanation of WHY NS is open in 3D but solved in 2D. It
  parallels Beal's "cubic-cubic-cubic is the unique signature where all
  classical structures coincide" and BSD's "Kolyvagin's Euler system is
  one-point-shaped." Each problem has a crisp structural reason the hard case
  is hard and the easy cases are easy — worth a comparative note.

- **Spark**: the supercriticality gap (subcritical energy ↔ critical $L^3$) is
  quantified by Tao's triple-log blowup rate. Is there an analog in the other
  two problems of a *quantified* residual difficulty? Beal has the counting
  heuristic (predicted monotonicity); BSD has the analytic-rank-≥4 ceiling.
  Maybe a "quantified obstruction" cross-page.

- **Spark (blowup side)**: Tao's averaged-NS blowup is the most credible
  evidence that true-NS blowup MIGHT exist. Analogs of a "modified problem that
  IS decidable" elsewhere: Beal → Fermat-Catalan (finiteness is a proven weaker
  statement); BSD → rank ≤1 is proven. Worth a comparative table of "weaker
  statement proven" / "modified problem decidable" across the three.
